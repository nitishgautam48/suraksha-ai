# Financial literacy basics: safe money habits

Summary prepared for Suraksha AI from public financial-literacy themes promoted by the National Centre for Financial Education (NCFE) and the Reserve Bank of India's financial literacy initiatives. This is general education, not investment advice.

## Emergency fund
An emergency fund is money kept aside, easily accessible, for unexpected events such as job loss or medical needs. A commonly used guideline is to build up three to six months of essential expenses over time. An emergency fund reduces the chance of panic decisions, high-cost borrowing or falling for "quick money" offers during a crisis.

## Budgeting and saving regularly
- Track income and expenses for a month to understand where money goes.
- Pay yourself first: set aside savings as soon as income arrives.
- Small, regular saving builds discipline and resilience over time.

## Understanding risk and return
Higher potential returns come with higher risk. Any offer of high returns with "no risk" is a contradiction and a warning sign. Understand any product before putting money in, and read official documents rather than relying on social-media claims.

## Borrowing responsibly
Borrow only from regulated lenders (banks and RBI-registered NBFCs). Understand the total cost of a loan, including fees. Avoid unregistered instant-loan apps that demand access to contacts and photos.

## Insurance and protection
Insurance is for protection against risk. Be cautious of anyone presenting insurance mainly as a high-return investment, and verify policies with the insurer directly.

## Getting advice
For personal investment advice, consult only a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser and verify their registration on sebi.gov.in. For grievances: banks (RBI Integrated Ombudsman, cms.rbi.org.in), securities market (SEBI SCORES), insurance (IRDAI Bima Bharosa).
