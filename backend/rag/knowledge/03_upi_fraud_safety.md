# UPI fraud safety guide

Summary prepared for Suraksha AI from public safety guidance by NPCI, RBI and the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C).

## The golden rule: you never need your UPI PIN to receive money
Entering your UPI PIN always means money is leaving your account. If someone says "enter your PIN to receive the refund/prize/payment", it is a scam.

## Collect-request fraud
Fraudsters send a "collect" or "request money" notification disguised as a refund or payment. Approving it with your PIN sends money to them. Decline requests you do not recognise.

## QR code fraud
Scanning a QR code is used to pay, not to receive. A buyer on a classifieds site who sends you a QR code "to receive payment" is trying to steal money.

## Fake payment screenshots
Scammers send edited screenshots showing they paid you, then ask you to "return the extra amount". Always check your bank statement or app, not a screenshot.

## Fake customer care
Search-engine results and social-media comments can list fake helpline numbers for UPI apps. Use help only from inside the official app or official website.

## Safe UPI habits
- Lock your phone and payment apps; never share your screen with strangers.
- Verify the payee name shown before entering your PIN.
- Set a daily transaction limit you are comfortable with.
- Turn on transaction alerts.
- If defrauded, immediately call 1930 (National Cyber Crime Helpline) and your bank. Quick reporting improves the chance that funds can be frozen.
